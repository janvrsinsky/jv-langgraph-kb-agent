# Customer Support Playbook, Acme Robotics

## Response commitments

Standard tier is next business day. Gold is a four-hour response during
customer working hours and carries a 99.5 percent uptime commitment on
FleetMind. Platinum is one hour and a dedicated technical account manager.
The clock measures our first substantive reply, not an automated receipt.

## What counts as downtime

FleetMind downtime is measured as the server being unable to assign tasks.
A site that keeps running on already-assigned work is not down. Planned
maintenance inside an agreed window is excluded, and the window must be agreed
in writing at least five working days ahead.

## Severity

Severity 1 is a stopped line or a safety concern. Severity 2 is degraded
throughput with a workaround. Severity 3 is everything else. Severity is set
by the customer's operational impact, not by how interesting the bug is, and
support may raise it but never lower it without the customer agreeing.

## Escalation path

Support engineer, then the on-call FleetMind engineer, then the controls team
for anything on the robot side, then the head of engineering. A severity 1 that
is still open after four hours is escalated automatically, whether or not
anyone asks.

## On-site service

Included in Gold and Platinum. Standard tier customers can buy a visit at day
rates. Firmware updates are included in every tier, which is the question that
comes up most often and is worth answering before it is asked.

## What we do not promise

We do not commit to a fix time, only to a response time and to continuous
effort on severity 1. Anyone who promises a fix window on a call has created a
commitment the company has not made, and that is the single most common source
of an unhappy renewal.

## Spare part logistics

Wear parts ship next day from Prague across Europe. Non-stocked assemblies are
roughly six weeks. Tell the customer the real number early; a six-week part
described as "soon" costs more trust than the delay itself.
